Why big cats are in trouble

Five of the six cats on our list are threatened. Here is what is happening, and what you can do about it.

How many are left?

For some big cats, the whole world population would fit inside one sports stadium.

The four biggest threats

  1. Losing their homes Forests and grasslands are being turned into farms, roads and towns.
    • Big cats are left in small patches of wild land that are cut off from each other.
    • In small patches it is harder to find a mate, so groups become weaker over time.
  2. Losing their food People hunt the same deer, antelope and wild pigs that big cats eat.
    • With less wild prey, big cats may turn to farm animals instead.
  3. Conflict with people When a big cat kills a farmer’s cows or goats, the cat is sometimes killed in return.
    • Stronger night-time pens for farm animals keep both animals and cats safe.
    • Some programs pay farmers back for animals they lose, so they don’t need to take revenge.
  4. Poaching and illegal trade Big cats are hunted illegally for their skins, bones and other body parts.
    • Cubs are sometimes taken from the wild to be sold as pets.
    • Ranger patrols and stronger laws help stop poachers and traders.

How you can help

  • At home
    • Learn about one big cat and teach a friend or family member.
    • Never buy souvenirs made from wild animals.
  • At school
    • Ask your teacher about a big cat project or assembly.
    • Hold a bake sale or fun run for a conservation group.
  • Online
    • Share facts from trusted sources such as the IUCN Red List (opens in a new tab).
    • Check where a fact came from before you share it.
  • With our club
    • Come to one of our upcoming events in Riverside.
    • Sign up as a volunteer and help us plan the next one.

Groups protecting big cats in the wild

These organizations work with scientists, rangers and local communities where big cats live:

  • Panthera: research and protection for all 40 wild cat species (opens in a new tab)
  • Snow Leopard Trust: working with mountain communities in Asia (opens in a new tab)
  • Cheetah Conservation Fund: protecting cheetahs in Namibia and beyond (opens in a new tab)
